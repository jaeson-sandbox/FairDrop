## Opening questions and evidence

- **Scope versus verification size:** the draft owns ten deferred findings across
  source, ZIP, discovery and coordinator cleanup. Yui favors separating directory
  safety from cleanup. Dana favors retaining the assigned story but implementing
  and verifying smaller dependency-ordered checkpoints. Neither position waives
  any finding; a story split would require explicit ownership/tracking changes.
- **Identity window:** `internal/stream/payload.go:prepareArchive` currently checks
  root metadata but retains only the path. The draft's retained root capability
  addresses Prepare-to-WriteTo replacement. It does not establish identity from
  the original Stage, nor snapshot contents. Vex wants those limits explicit so
  the safety claim does not exceed the planned implementation.
- **Reader lifetime:** `internal/source/source.go:borrowedContent` has an
  unsynchronized boolean and reads through a handle closed after visitor return.
  Boundary calls for deterministic read-in-flight/visitor-return/close tests,
  not only a race-free flag or sequential retained-reader test. Synchronization
  must not be represented as an ability to interrupt a stuck OS read.
- **Timeout honesty:** `internal/transfer/coordinator.go:stopServerBounded` records
  an inner Stop error and returns nil when that call completed within the outer
  bound. Grumbal insists the draft's inner-timeout propagation be a separate
  behavioral test: increasing the outer bound alone can make an unconfirmed
  cleanup look successful. Ordinary cleanup diagnostics and unproven quiescence
  need an explicit distinction.
- **Meaning of portable names:** rejecting unsafe segments does not prove that
  every archive extracts losslessly on every receiver. Case/normalization
  collisions are not covered by the proposed stateless segment predicate. Do
  not broaden the claim or add a whole-tree collision index without a decision.

At the opening discussion these were proposals and no production code/tests
changed. The subsequent owner approval above authorizes the implementation
sequence, not a waiver of tests or an expanded snapshot/receiver guarantee.
