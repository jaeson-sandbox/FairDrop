- (decision) Confirmed UX sources: canonical SPEC, architecture spine, Phase contracts, final epics and stories, and the corrected original FairDrop spec. Review and party artifacts are excluded as process evidence.
- (decision) UX north star: FairDrop should evoke the effortless device-to-device experience of Apple AirDrop while extending that ease to Windows and browser-receiving devices. The core gesture is dropping one file or folder into the client and transferring it with extremely low friction through QR code or another simple handoff.
- (decision) Visual and interaction posture: support both light and dark modes; feel low-profile, compact, and popup-like rather than like a conventional standalone application; remain minimal because the premise is simple; transitions and interactions should feel fluid, smooth, polished, and glitch-free.
- (assumption) The popup-like quality will be achieved through compact layout, restrained hierarchy, and transient-utility behavior inside the required standard OS window chrome; the architecture prohibits a frameless window.
- (event) Vision brain dump closed; user reported no additional vision input before continuing.
- (assumption) Product stakes are treated as a polished consumer utility, not a regulated product, based on the stated goal of a broadly approachable AirDrop-like experience. Revisit if distribution or compliance scope changes.
- (decision) Form factor is multi-surface: a compact native Windows/macOS sender window plus an ordinary modern browser download surface on the receiving device. No custom receiver application is in scope.
- (change) Broader product vision: FairDrop should become an OS-agnostic AirDrop-like bridge for mixed-device environments, making Mac-to-Windows and Windows-to-Mac transfers nearly as easy as AirDrop and ultimately supporting any-device-to-any-device exchange.
- (assumption) Native iOS and other mobile sending are treated as considered future extensions, not V1 commitments. V1 remains the approved Windows/macOS desktop sender with same-LAN browser receiving; UX and protocol decisions should avoid unnecessarily blocking future native clients.
- (decision) V1 surface boundary confirmed: Windows/macOS native sender to any same-LAN device with a compatible modern browser. Native iOS/Android sending and broader any-device-to-any-device exchange remain explicit roadmap intent, not current V1 scope.
- (decision) Platform-ordering principle: the experience should make source and destination OS feel irrelevant. Anchor the first journey in Windows-to-iPhone; validate Mac-to-Windows next using the same V1 desktop-sender/browser-receiver model; treat iPhone-to-Windows as a later native mobile-sender capability.
- (decision) Primary named journey — Jaeson receives a folder of PDFs from his dad on a Windows PC and wants the PDFs on his iPhone for reading on the go. He opens compact FairDrop, drops the folder, confirms its recognizable name and ZIP expectation, scans the staged QR code with the iPhone, accepts the browser download, watches the sender show honest ZIP streaming progress, and reaches Done. Climax: the iPhone download begins immediately after the scan with no account, pairing ritual, or OS-compatibility decision. The folder arrives as a ZIP that iOS Files can open so Jaeson can access the PDFs away from the PC.
